The 3 jobs Polytechnitis will not touch

Three categories get declined outright by Polytechnitis: glazing, garage door motors, and garage doors themselves. Tensioned springs make a garage door a job for a specialist who turns up with the correct parts already on the van. Glass belongs to a glazier.

A refusal is worth hearing early, because it saves you a visit that was never going to fix anything. Take a sliding door. Cracked glass in it means ringing a glazier. A door that simply will not run along its track is a different problem, normally worn rollers or a track packed with grit, and that is ordinary handyman work. Telling those two apart happens at the quote.

Work that needs no licence in the ACT

Seven kinds of work need no trade licence in the ACT: mounting, assembly, gutter work, tiling, painting, carpentry that carries no structural load, and general repairs. Polytechnitis covers all 7.

Plumbing and electrical sit on the far side of that line. Size makes no difference to it. Moving a tap calls for a licensed plumber, and changing a light fitting calls for a licensed electrician. In both cases the team gives you the name of the trade to ring instead of starting something it cannot finish.

How the Canberra year sets the job list

Handyman work in the ACT tracks the weather more closely than people expect. Timber takes up and gives off moisture through cold winters and dry summers, which is why a door that closed sweetly in autumn starts to stick by the middle of winter and wants easing, planing and re-hanging. Once the leaves drop, gutters and downpipes fill faster here than they do in softer coastal climates, so that work stacks up between March and May.

Canberra in brief

Australia's capital city, and the seat of the Australian Capital Territory, Canberra was drawn up on paper before it was built. What came of that is not a single continuous urban core but a string of separate town centres.
